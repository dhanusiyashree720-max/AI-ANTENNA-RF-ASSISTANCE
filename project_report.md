CAPSTONE 15 — AI Antenna and RF Concepts Learning Assistant

1. Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) can support engineering education by explaining technical concepts, generating examples, and helping students practise numerical problems. However, AI may produce incorrect formulas, unit errors, or invented values.

This project investigates how AI can teach antenna and Radio Frequency (RF) fundamentals while ensuring that its explanations and calculations are verified using reliable engineering references.

2. Problem Statement

How can AI teach antenna and RF basics while avoiding incorrect formulas and invented values?

3. Objectives

- Explain five important antenna and RF topics.
- Collect reliable technical reference materials.
- Generate beginner and advanced explanations using AI.
- Verify formulas, definitions, units, and numerical results.
- Solve eight numerical problems and check each manually.
- Document errors and corrections.
- Prepare a glossary and a ten-question quiz.
- Generate and verify educational diagrams.
- Automate link-budget calculations using a spreadsheet.
- Evaluate AI limitations in engineering calculations.

4. Topics Covered

4.1 Dipole Antenna

A dipole antenna is a conductor-based antenna commonly used for transmitting and receiving electromagnetic waves. A half-wave dipole has a total length approximately equal to half the wavelength under idealized conditions.

4.2 Antenna Gain

Antenna gain describes how effectively an antenna directs radiated power in a particular direction compared with a reference antenna. Gain is commonly expressed in dBi when referenced to an isotropic antenna.

4.3 Impedance Matching

Impedance matching reduces reflections between connected RF components. It improves power transfer when the load impedance is appropriately matched to the source or transmission line.

4.4 Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR)

VSWR describes the standing-wave condition caused by reflections on a transmission line. For a passive load under normal conditions, its value ranges from 1 to infinity. A value of 1 indicates no reflection.

4.5 RF Link Budget

An RF link budget accounts for transmitted power, antenna gains, path losses, and other relevant gains and losses to estimate received power.

5. AI Tools Used

The planned tools include ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Perplexity, Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel, Canva, and GitHub.

The tools used and their actual contributions will be recorded in the final report.

6. Reference Materials

Reliable references will include antenna and transmission-line textbooks, recognized engineering standards where relevant, and authoritative technical documentation.

For every formula, the final report will identify its source, assumptions, units, and applicable conditions.

7. Formula and Calculation Verification

Each AI-generated formula will be checked against a reference. Numerical results will be recalculated independently, with units and intermediate steps recorded.

Eight numerical problems and their verified solutions will be included in the completed report.

8. AI Error Log

An error log will record any observed incorrect equation, unit error, unsupported value, or misleading explanation. Each entry will include the original output, the correction, the supporting reference, and the verification method.

If no error is observed during a particular test, this will be recorded honestly rather than inventing an error.

9. Glossary and Quiz

A glossary of important RF terms and a ten-question quiz with a verified answer key will be prepared.

10. Diagrams

Educational diagrams will illustrate antenna structure, radiation patterns, impedance matching, VSWR, and RF link-budget concepts. Each diagram will be reviewed for technical correctness.

11. Automation

A spreadsheet-based RF link-budget calculator will accept relevant input values and calculate received power using a documented formula. Units, assumptions, and test cases will be included.

12. Before-and-After Analysis

AI-generated explanations will be compared with verified explanations. The comparison will examine correctness, clarity, unit consistency, source support, and the quality of numerical reasoning.

13. Limitations of AI

AI can generate plausible but incorrect technical information. It may confuse units, omit assumptions, misapply formulas, or provide unsupported numerical values. AI-generated engineering content must therefore be independently verified before use.

14. Conclusion

This project demonstrates a verification-focused approach to using AI for antenna and RF education. Reliable references, independent calculations, documented prompts, and spreadsheet testing help make AI-assisted learning more transparent and dependable.

15. Project Status

This is the initial report draft. Verified numerical results, reference details, the error log, quiz, diagrams, automation test results, and final analysis will be added as the project progresses.
